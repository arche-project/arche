# The gaps for a self-reliant household

> A review of the catalogue as of 11 September 2026 (248 resources, 20 bundles) from the point of view
> of someone living **permanently** outside the system, not someone riding out a 72-hour event.
> Blunt on purpose. Français : [TROUS-AUTONOMIE.md](../fr/TROUS-AUTONOMIE.md)

## 0. The distinction that structures everything

A **prepper** prepares for an event: stock up, sit it out, resume normal life.
A **self-reliant household** replaces functions — water, heat, calories, care, repair — permanently,
with seasons, wear, breakdowns and other people.

Those are two different catalogues. Ours today is 80 % a well-equipped prepper catalogue and 20 % a
self-reliance catalogue. The numbers say so plainly.

Three more figures, just as telling:

- **68 of 248 resources** are visible to the *low-tech* profile. Someone without reliable electricity —
  that is, every self-reliant household in February — sees a quarter of the catalogue.
- **56 of 248 resources** contain any French. For a project whose core audience is francophone, that
  is a structural problem, not a detail.
- **34 of 248 resources** are marked `essential`. So the newcomer has no idea where to start.

**This is not a complaint about robotics**: LeRobot and the printable arms are excellent and they serve
Robot Academy. But in *this* catalogue they must drop to `optional` and leave the `novice` profile,
otherwise a beginner downloads 300 GB of which 40 % will never feed them.

## 2. The gaps by domain, in the order they kill

The rule of threes sets the order: 3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter in severe cold,
3 days without water, 3 weeks without food. Our catalogue is built in the opposite order.

### 2.1 Water — C1, the most serious gap

We have: *zimgit-water* (20 MB), a water-quality sensor, a hydraulic ram, one Appropedia page.
We have nothing on: **lifting** water without electricity (rope pump, treadle pump, a properly sized
ram), **storing** it (how many m³, buried or above ground, winter freezing, algae, turnover),
**catching** it (sizing a roof, first-flush diversion, guttering), **testing** it without a lab (strip
kits, what they detect and above all what they miss), **wells** (digging, lining, protecting the
wellhead from surface ingress), **greywater** (reed beds, constructed wetlands), and **winter**
(burying below the frost line, draining down).

**To do:** an entry for the `rope pump` (Appropedia, CC BY-SA, the most-copied pump in the world),
the water chapters of *Practical Action* and CD3WD, an emergency WASH manual (Sphere covers part of
it), and a printable sizing sheet — now delivered as sheet 08. Ask Kiwix for a ZIM of the **SSWM
Toolbox** (Sustainable Sanitation and Water Management) if the licence allows.

### 2.2 Heat and shelter — C1

Two resources. This is the most dangerous gap after water, because improvised heating **kills fast**:
carbon monoxide, chimney fires, flue collapse.

Missing: insulation (orders of magnitude, local materials — straw, hemp, wool, earth), **stove and
flue** (draught, sweeping, clearance to combustibles, CO alarms), masonry heaters and rocket stoves
(Low-tech Lab and OSE cover this, but their ZIMs are not built yet), framing and roofing (fixing a
roof before winter), masonry and lime, damp and mould (respiratory health), windows and shutters,
passive solar, and the **root cellar** (storing without electricity — which is also a food subject).

**To do:** a printable "heating without killing anyone" sheet (CO, sweeping, clearances); entries for
*Build It Solar*, *Appropedia construction*, the CD3WD construction chapters; and push the Low-tech
Lab ZIM recipe (already awaiting permission) — that is where the best French content is.

### 2.3 Calories and nutrition — C1, the most underestimated gap

We have 36 "agriculture" entries: tools, farm-management software, plant databases. We have **no**
resource that answers the only question that matters: *how much land, time and seed does it take to
feed one person for one year, and what will run short?*

Self-reliant households almost always fail on the same four points:

1. **Calories**: physical farm work demands 3 000–4 000 kcal/day, not 2 000. Vegetable gardens produce
   vitamins, not energy. You need starches.
2. **Fats**: the hardest thing to produce in a temperate climate (rapeseed or sunflower oil means a
   press; otherwise animal fat). Almost nobody plans for it.
3. **Salt**: 3–5 kg per person per year, impossible to produce inland, imperishable, trivial to store.
   The perfect blind spot.
4. **The spring hungry gap**: March to May, the stores are finished and nothing is harvested yet.
   Historically this is the famine window, and no resource in the catalogue mentions it.

Also missing: cereals (sowing, threshing, winnowing, **milling**, sourdough bread), dairy (milking,
rennet, cheese, butter), slaughter and butchery, long curing, seed (quantities and **minimum
population sizes** to avoid inbreeding depression), rotations and fertility without inputs, vitamin C
in winter (scurvy: sauerkraut, rosehips, potatoes), B12.

**To do:** sheet 08, "the numbers of self-reliance" (delivered with this document); FAO entries (grain
storage, on-farm processing), *Wikibooks Cookbook*, the CD3WD food chapters, and a dedicated
"seed: quantities and populations" entry built from Practical Plants and the seed-saving networks.

### 2.4 Tools, repair and metalwork — C2

The catalogue knows how to 3D-print and how to solder electronics. It does not know how to **sharpen a
blade**, **rehang a handle**, **rivet**, **forge a simple tool**, **salvage steel**, or choose a
lubricant, an adhesive, a fastener. The underlying question is: *what repairs the repair tools?*
Survivor Library (235 GB of 19th-century English) is the only current answer, and it is both too big
and too indirect.

**To do:** extract a "minimum workshop" selection from Survivor Library and Appropedia; entries on
forging and sharpening; a printable "the 20 tools that repair everything else".

### 2.5 Health over the long run — C2

We are strong on emergencies (first aid, obstetrics, IMCI, dental, plants). What is missing is the
**steady state**: chronic disease when the pharmacy closes (hypertension, diabetes, thyroid — with the
honesty to say what has **no** substitute: insulin cannot be replaced), **glasses** (uncorrected
myopia is a major disability, and dioptres can be stockpiled), collective hygiene (lice, scabies,
fleas), wound infection and antibiotic use (we document prevention, not manufacture), winter
deficiencies, and **death** — what to do with a body, what the law says, what a group does. Nobody
writes about it and everyone will face it.

### 2.6 Animals — C2 (partly filled)

Veterinary care was added yesterday. Still missing: **fodder** (making hay, quantities per animal per
winter), breeding and seasons, milking, slaughter and butchery, hides and leather, **draught animals**
(a horse replaces the tractor when the diesel stops), and the awkward arithmetic: a hen eats ~45 kg of
grain a year, so six hens per person consume almost one human's worth of cereal land. Animals only pay
when they eat what we cannot.

### 2.7 Transport and mobility — C3, entirely absent

Zero resources. Missing: **bicycle** repair (the bicycle is the most efficient machine ever built and
the first thing to run again), car mechanics without a garage, fuel storage and ageing, animal
traction, trailers, wood gas.

### 2.8 Textiles, hygiene, daily life — C3, entirely absent

Zero resources. Sewing, patching, knitting, footwear, soap (ash and fat), laundry, menstrual
management, washable nappies, heating bathwater. These are the subjects that separate surviving from
living, and they are invisible in every survivalist catalogue — because they have historically been
women's work. That is a cultural blind spot, not a technical one.

### 2.9 Group, decisions, transmission — C2

Four resources, three of them about acute crisis. Nothing about duration: dividing labour, collective
decision-making, neighbour disputes, **organised** home schooling (we have textbooks, not a
curriculum), welcoming newcomers, and passing on physical skills — which are not learned from a ZIM.

### 2.10 Administrative and legal — C2

One resource. Yet settling somewhere is a very concrete administrative path: buying land, reading the
zoning plan, filing for a permit, declaring a well, a septic system, beehives, livestock, dealing with
tax and insurance, home schooling (which is inspected), available support schemes. The Service-Public
ZIM is still an open decision — and it is now the most valuable of the pending ones.

## 3. The methodological gaps (worse than the content gaps)

### 3.1 There is not a single number — C1

The catalogue explains *how*, never *how much*. But self-reliance is a sizing problem: litres per day,
m² per person, cords per winter, kWh, kilos of seed, m³ of cistern. Without orders of magnitude you
build too small and find out in February. **This is the easiest gap to fill and the most useful one**
→ sheet 08, delivered with this document.

### 3.2 Nothing is seasonal — C2

A self-reliant household does not ask "how do I can vegetables", it asks "it is 15 November, what
should already be done". The catalogue is a library, not a calendar. A **self-reliance calendar**
(12 pages, one per month, temperate climate) is cheap to produce and would change how the whole thing
is used.

### 3.3 Reading is not knowing how — C2

Grafting, milking, shoeing, applying a tourniquet: documentation does not transfer the gesture. The
project has to own the **practice** side — "one skill a month", a logbook, and above all pointing
people towards those who already know (associations, farms, fablabs) while the network still exists.

### 3.4 The catalogue does not know its user — C3

The `bunker` profile sees 247 of 248 resources. That is not a profile, it is the absence of sorting.
There should be a distinct **`self-reliant`** profile — permanent, seasonal, low-tech compatible,
French first — whose core is water + heat + calories + health + group, and which hides robotics, heavy
AI and dev tooling.

## 4. What I propose, by return on effort

1. **Sheet 08, "the numbers of self-reliance"** — delivered today. One page, no dependencies, useful
   immediately, and it is gap number one.
2. **A `self-reliant` profile** in `profiles.yaml`, plus demoting robotics to `optional` and out of the
   novice profile. An hour of work; it changes the entire experience.
3. **Water and shelter**: ten verified entries (rope pump, SSWM, Build It Solar, CD3WD/Practical Action
   chapters) plus the "heating without killing anyone" sheet (CO, sweeping).
4. **The self-reliance calendar** (12 printable pages).
5. **Textiles, hygiene, transport**: three sheets and a dozen entries — the cost is low and the absence
   is total.
6. **Service-Public / Légifrance**: settle that decision. It is the only content missing from the
   "settling legally" half of the project.

What I do not propose is adding more sources. We have 248, of which 30 have uncertain licences and
many have URLs to fix. The next gain is not in volume, it is in **structure, numbers and French**.
