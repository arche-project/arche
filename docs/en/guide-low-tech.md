# Low-tech guide — knowledge on paper

> Français : [guide-low-tech.md](../fr/guide-low-tech.md)

## Why a paper profile

Hard drives fail. Batteries drain. Screens crack. File formats become unreadable. Paper reads by
candlelight, can be lent, annotated, survives humidity if protected, and needs no skill to "boot".
Arche's *low-tech* profile assumes **electronics will fail at some point** and that a foundation
must exist that doesn't depend on them. It also serves people who have no computer, or want none.

## What the profile selects

`arche plan --profile lowtech` lists only resources marked *printable* or with a paper edition, and
hides everything that only makes sense on a screen (AI, Git repos, toolchains). The result is a few
tens of GB of PDFs, to print by priority:

| Priority | Document | Pages ≈ | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Where There Is No Doctor* (Hesperian) | 500 | the community-health reference, written for untrained readers |
| 1 | IFRC first aid guidelines | 300 | life-saving actions, protocols |
| 1 | [Arche sheets](../printables/en/): water, fire, preservation, knots, signals | 20 | the essentials on one page each, laminate-able |
| 2 | *Where There Is No Dentist* (Hesperian) | 400 | tooth pain is the first untreated emergency in isolation |
| 2 | Sphere Handbook | 460 | organising a group: water, sanitation, shelter, food, quantities per person |
| 2 | Appropedia / Low-tech Lab selection | 200 | rocket stove, solar dryer, sand filter, dry latrines |
| 3 | Wikibooks extracts (gardening, canning) | 150 | |
| 3 | Blacksmithing, tanning, weaving manuals (Survivor Library) | varies | 19th-century, very long — don't print everything |

This table is a **proposal**: the catalog carries `printable: {available, pages_estimate}`; you choose.

## Print and bind to last

- **Paper** 80–90 g, double-sided, 2 cm margins for binding.
- **Binding**: plastic or metal spiral (lies flat, survives use); one-page sheets in **laminated
  pockets** in a binder.
- **Protection**: airtight box + desiccant sachets. One copy in two different places.
- **Index**: print each manual's table of contents separately and keep it in front.
- **Cost**: 1,500 double-sided pages ≈ €40–60 at a copy shop, less on a home laser printer (toner
  keeps for years, unlike ink).

## What paper doesn't replace

Full-text search. Two answers: (1) print **indexes** and tables of contents carefully; (2) keep
**one** small electronic backup (a phone, or a Pi + battery + 20 W solar panel) with the digital
version — that's Arche's "old laptop" profile, 150 GB.

## Passing knowledge on without electricity

Paper is useless if nobody knows how to use it. The low-tech profile has a social side:
**drills** (purify 10 L of water, apply a tourniquet, light a fire in the rain), a **logbook** of
what was tried and what worked, **one reference person** per domain. See
[BLIND-SPOTS.md](BLIND-SPOTS.md), "transmission".

## Paper sources to buy beforehand

Some references don't exist as free PDFs or are far better bound: the US Army *Survival* manual
(FM 21-76, public domain, pocket editions); a regional medicinal/edible plant guide with photos; a
paper **road atlas** of your region and a **1:25,000 topographic map** of your 20 km; a local
sowing calendar; Hesperian's print editions (women's and children's health). None are in the
catalog (rights) — it's a shopping list.
