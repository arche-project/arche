# 07 · What kills: mushrooms and plants

**Laminate it and keep it in the foraging basket.** This sheet is not for deciding what to eat: it is for ruling things out. You do not learn foraging from a sheet — you learn it from someone who knows, then you get your harvest checked.

## The rule that matters: the DELAY

The only criterion that sorts reliably, and the doctor's first question.

| First symptoms | What it means |
|---|---|
| **less than 6 h** after the meal | usually benign (digestive upset), but still call |
| **more than 6 h** after the meal | **LIFE-THREATENING** — liver or kidney destruction already under way. Hospital immediately, even if you feel better |
| **2 to 21 days** after | orellanus syndrome: kidneys being destroyed silently. Hospital |

**The deadly trap**: after the violent digestive phase comes a 12–24 h "remission" where you feel cured. The liver is dying. Never stay home during that lull.

## The mushrooms that kill in Europe

**Phalloides syndrome — over 90% of deaths. One cap is enough.** Death cap (*Amanita phalloides*), spring amanita (*A. verna*), destroying angel (*A. virosa*), **small Lepiota** (any under 10 cm), funeral bell (*Galerina marginata*). → **White** gills, a **ring** on the stem, a **volva** (sac) at the base you must dig up to see.

**Orellanus syndrome — 2 to 21 days delay, kidneys destroyed.** *Cortinarius orellanus*, *C. rubellus*. Tawny-rust tones, cobweb-like veil in young specimens.

**Gyromitra syndrome.** False morel (*Gyromitra esculenta*), confused with morels: **brain-like** cap, not pitted; stem solid, not hollow.

**Other severe.** *Amanita proxima* (kidneys), *Tricholoma equestre* (muscle destruction), *Pleurocybella porrigens*, *Entoloma sinuatum*, *Omphalotus olearius*, Satan's bolete.

### The confusions that kill

| You think you're picking… | You are actually picking… | The detail that saves you |
|---|---|---|
| field mushroom (Agaricus) | spring amanita / destroying angel | gills **pink then brown** in Agaricus, **always white** in Amanita; **dig up the stem**: volva = Amanita |
| green russula, tricholoma | death cap | ring + volva; never cut at ground level |
| parasol mushroom | deadly small Lepiota | parasol = **large** (> 15 cm), snakeskin-patterned stem, **sliding** ring |
| sheathed woodtuft (on stumps) | funeral bell | when in doubt: **eat no small brown mushroom growing on wood** |
| chanterelle | Cortinarius, jack-o'-lantern | chanterelle: **forked** decurrent folds, not true gills, apricot smell |
| morel | false morel | morel: **pitted** cap and **hollow** stem |

## The plants that kill

| Deadly | Confused with | The detail |
|---|---|---|
| **autumn crocus** | wild garlic, wild leek | wild garlic **smells of garlic** when crushed; crocus does not |
| **foxglove** | comfrey, borage | alternate leaves in foxglove; never infuse an unknown leaf |
| **hemlock water-dropwort** (deadliest in France) | wild celery, parsnip, parsley | yellowish **finger-like** roots, strong smell; grows with its feet in water |
| **poison hemlock** | parsley, wild carrot, chervil | stem **blotched red-purple**, smell of mouse urine |
| **white hellebore** | yellow gentian | **alternate** leaves in hellebore, **opposite** in gentian |
| **yew** (seed and leaf) | — | the red aril is harmless, **the seed inside kills** |
| **belladonna, datura, aconite, castor bean, oleander** | — | no reason to touch them |

## Tests that do not work — they have killed people

Silver spoon, coin, onion turning black, slugs or animals eating it, taste, pleasant smell, "it grows near a good spot", thorough cooking, drying, salting. **None.** The death cap tastes good, slugs love it, and cooking does not destroy its toxins.

## The eight rules

1. Eat only what you can **name**, with at least two criteria, one of them seen **underground** (dig up the whole stem).
2. **Get it checked**: a pharmacist (free in France) or the local mycological society. While the network holds, that is the best resource in the world.
3. Pick **whole**, in a basket (never a plastic bag), separate species, don't mix in what the children collected.
4. **Photograph** the harvest before cooking and **keep a raw sample refrigerated**: it saves lives in the emergency room.
5. Don't rely on **identification apps** to decide to eat (ANSES says so explicitly). They rule out, they don't validate.
6. **Cook at least 20 minutes**, never raw, never reheated repeatedly. Small amounts.
7. No wild mushrooms for **children**, **pregnant women**, the **elderly** or the frail.
8. At the slightest symptom: emergency services, or a poison control centre. Give the meal time, the symptom time, and show the photo.

## Off-grid: the real question

A wild mushroom provides **20 to 40 kcal per 100 g** — half an apple for a morning of foraging, at lethal risk. In a food shortage it is not a food source, it is a high-risk pleasure. Calories are in starches, nuts, fats and proteins: see sheet 04 (preservation) and the agriculture section. Learning mushrooms is first and foremost about **not** poisoning yourself — and not poisoning others when you cook for a group.
