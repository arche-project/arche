# 08 · The numbers of self-reliance

**Print double-sided, laminate, pin it above the workbench.** The rest of the library explains *how*.
This sheet says *how much*. It is the only place in Arche that gives orders of magnitude — and an
order of magnitude that is wrong by half is a winter that ends in February.

> **Read this first.** These figures are for a temperate climate (Western Europe, northern US,
> southern Canada), average soil, hand tools and animal or human power. They are **orders of
> magnitude**, not guarantees: ±30 % depending on soil, season, technique and you. A number tells you
> whether you are off by a factor of 2 or a factor of 10 — it never replaces measuring at home.
> **Measure, then correct in the margin.**

---

## 1 · Water — the 3-day constraint

| Use | Per person per day |
|---|---|
| bare survival (drinking only) | **2–3 L** (0.5–0.8 gal) |
| humanitarian minimum (drink, cook, wash) — Sphere standard | **15 L** |
| frugal self-reliant living | **30–50 L** |
| household with shower and laundry | 80–150 L |

**Animals** (per head per day): hen 0.3 L · goat 5–10 L · pig 10–20 L · **dairy cow 50–100 L**.
A cow drinks more than six people: the herd decides the water supply, not the other way round.

**Rainwater harvesting** — the formula to remember:

> **litres/year = roof footprint (m²) × rainfall (mm/year) × 0.8**

A 100 m² roof at 800 mm/year → **64 000 L/year** (64 m³). That sounds like plenty — but it falls at
the wrong times. Size the cistern on **the longest dry spell**, never on the annual total:
4 people × 40 L × 90 days = **14 m³**. Plan for 10–20 m³ for a household, not 1 000 L.
Divert the first flush: 0.5–1 L per m² of roof. Asbestos, lead, bitumen shingle or treated roofing: not for drinking.

**Spring or well**: 1 L/min = 1 440 L/day = comfortably one household. Measure it with a bucket and a
watch **in September** (low water), never in April.

**Dry storage**: 3 L/person/day × 15 days = **45 L per person** to survive two weeks with nothing.
Everything beyond that is a filtration problem (sheet 01).

## 2 · Calories and land — the 3-month constraint

| Situation | Per adult per day |
|---|---|
| sedentary, heated house | 2 000–2 400 kcal |
| **manual farm work** | **3 000–4 000 kcal** |
| hard labour in the cold | up to 4 500 kcal |

**The classic mistake** is sizing on 2 000 kcal. In self-reliance you work physically every day: budget
**3 500 kcal** for an active adult. A household of 4 (2 working adults, 2 children) =
**~11 000 kcal/day = 4 million kcal/year**.

**Food density** (kcal per 100 g, as stored):
wheat/rice/pasta **340** · dry pulses **330** · potato 77 · **oil 900** · lard 890 · nuts 650 ·
honey/sugar 400 · milk powder 500 · cabbage, courgette, lettuce **15–30**.

> A vegetable garden does not feed you; it keeps you healthy. Calories are in **starches, fats and
> oilseeds**. 10 kg of courgettes = 2 000 kcal = half a litre of oil.

**Land — for *one* person, one year:**

| What you want | Area | Assumed skill |
|---|---|---|
| fresh vegetables plus some starch | 200–400 m² | intensive market gardening, well practised |
| **a complete diet, buying nothing** | **1 000–2 500 m²** | garden + grain + losses + fallow |
| the same, with animals and their fodder | **0.5–1 ha** (1.2–2.5 acres) | pasture + range |

The 400 m²/person figure from biointensive schools is a **record**, set by very experienced growers on
built-up soil in a favourable climate. Budget two or three times that for your first decade.

**Reference yields** (no synthetic fertiliser, hand worked):
potato **15–25 t/ha** · wheat **2.5–4 t/ha** · dry beans 1–2 t/ha · winter squash 20–30 t/ha ·
Jerusalem artichoke 20–40 t/ha. In calories the potato is unbeatable (≈ 15 million kcal/ha); wheat
stores ten times better. **You need both.**

## 3 · The three traps nobody calculates

### Fats — the real gap
Requirement: **60–80 g/day**, i.e. **~25 kg of fat or oil per person per year**. It is the first thing
to run out in a temperate climate, and it is what makes everything else worth eating.

| Source | What it yields | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **mature walnut tree** | 20–50 kg nuts → **12–30 kg oil** | 2–3 trees feed a family. **Plant now: 8–10 years to bear** |
| rapeseed / sunflower | ~1 t oil/ha | 250–400 m² per person **plus a press** |
| pig (100–120 kg live) | **15–25 kg lard** plus the meat | 300–400 kg of feed |
| cow or goat milk | butter ≈ 4 % of the milk | one cow = 30–40 kg butter/year |

Add **3–5 kg of fat per person per year for soap** (1 kg fat ≈ 1.3 kg soap).
A family of four: **~120 kg of fat a year, all in**. Look that number in the face.

### Salt — five euros that save everything
**3–5 kg per person per year** (1–2 kg physiological, the rest for curing meat and vegetables).
Unobtainable inland, essential for preserving, **imperishable and almost free**.
Storing **10 kg per person** is the best survival-per-euro ratio in this whole library.
Dry curing: 25–35 g salt per kg of meat. Brine: 200–250 g salt per litre of water.

### The hungry gap — March to June
The stores are empty and nothing is ripe yet. **Size your stock for 12 months, not for the harvest.**
What crosses the gap: grain, pulses, oil, salt, winter cabbage and leeks, clamped roots, lacto-ferments,
canned goods. Nothing else does. Keep **10–15 % of the harvest as seed** — before eating, not after.

## 4 · Heat and firewood — the 6-month constraint

**1 stère** (1 m³ stacked, 1 m logs ≈ 0.28 cord) of **seasoned hardwood** ≈ 1 700–1 900 kWh gross ≈
**1 300–1 450 kWh useful** in a stove. Wet wood (> 25 % moisture): half the heat, and a chimney fire eventually.

| House | Per winter |
|---|---|
| old, uninsulated, 120 m² | **15–25 stères** (4–7 cords) |
| decently insulated | 6–10 stères |
| very well insulated / masonry heater | 2–5 stères |
| **cooking only**, on wood | 0.5–1.5 t/person/year (a rocket stove uses 3× less than an open fire) |

**Coppice yield: 3–8 stères per hectare per year** on rotation (chestnut, hornbeam, black locust: cut
every 15–30 years). Heating an old house therefore needs **2–5 hectares of managed woodland** — not a
copse. This is the number that makes people move house.

**Seasoning takes 18–24 months**, split, under a ventilated roof, off the ground. So you need **two
years standing ahead of you at all times**: the wood you burn and the wood that is drying. Cutting in
November to burn in January does not work.

**What actually kills**: **carbon monoxide** (no smell, no colour) and chimney fires. Sweep the flue
**twice a year**, once during the heating season. **One battery CO alarm per heated room** (€15, 10
years) — and spare batteries. Never a brazier, barbecue or generator indoors.

## 5 · Electricity — frugal, not heroic

| What you run | Per day |
|---|---|
| **an Arche node** (mini-PC + disk + Wi-Fi, 10–20 W) | **0.2–0.5 kWh** |
| + LED lighting, radio, phone charging | 0.3–0.8 kWh |
| + a laptop for a few hours | 0.5–1 kWh |
| frugal off-grid home (pump, no fridge) | 2–4 kWh |
| + an A+++ fridge | +0.5–1 kWh |

**Size solar on December, never on the annual average.** In France or the northern US, 1 kWp of panels
makes ~1 100–1 300 kWh/year — but **4–5 kWh/day in June and only 0.4–0.9 kWh/day in December**.
For a steady 2 kWh/day you need **3–4 kWp**, not 1.5.

**Battery**: aim for **3 days of autonomy**. 2 kWh/day → 6 kWh usable → ~7.5 kWh of LiFePO4 (80 % depth
of discharge) or ~12 kWh of lead-acid (50 %). Lead dies in 3–5 years if you run it flat; LiFePO4 lasts
10–15 years but must not be charged below 0 °C.

**The minimum kit that works**: one 100 W panel + one 100 Ah 12 V battery (1.2 kWh) + an MPPT
controller = light, radio, phones, and **the Arche library readable every day**. About €300. That is
the first purchase, not the last.

**Losses people forget**: inverter −10 %, cabling −3 %, battery round trip −10 to −20 %.
Budget **30 % lost** between the panel and the bulb.

## 6 · Animals — it is a fodder problem, not an animal problem

| Animal | What it gives | What it eats |
|---|---|---|
| **laying hen** | 150–250 eggs/year (falls off after year 2) | **45 kg grain/year** |
| 6 hens | ~1 000 eggs | **270 kg grain ≈ 0.1 ha of wheat** |
| **dairy goat** | 1.5–3 L/day for 8–10 months | 2–3 kg dry matter/day |
| **dairy cow** | 10–20 L/day | 15–25 kg DM/day → **~1 ha** + **2.5–3 t of hay** for winter |
| **pig** | 100–120 kg live → 70–80 kg meat + 20 kg fat | 300–400 kg feed (3–4 kg per kg of gain) |
| **rabbits** (1 buck, 3 does) | 60–100 kg meat/year | forage + 100–150 kg pellets or grain |

Hay yield: **3–6 t/ha**. One cow = **0.5–1 ha of pasture** plus hay for winter.
A pig converts surplus; it does not create food. With no scraps and no acorns, it eats your wheat.
**Hens and rabbits are the only animals a household can really feed without land in grain.**

## 7 · Seed — how much to keep, how many plants

**Quantities**: wheat **150–200 kg/ha** sown (keep 10–15 % of the harvest) ·
potato **2–2.5 t/ha** planted (1 kg planted → ~10 kg harvested, so keep 10–12 %) · beans 80–120 kg/ha.

**Minimum population to save seed without inbreeding depression** — this is where people fail:

| Species | Minimum plants |
|---|---|
| tomato, bean, lettuce, pea, wheat (selfers) | **20** |
| squash, cucumber | 20–50 |
| brassicas, carrot, onion, beet | **40–80** |
| **maize, rye, buckwheat (outcrossers)** | **200** |

**Seed longevity** (dry, cool, dark): onion, parsnip, leek **1–2 years** · carrot, pepper 2–3 ·
bean, pea, wheat 3–4 · brassica, tomato, beet 4–6 · squash, cucumber 5–8.
Rule of thumb: **every 5 °C cooler or 1 % drier doubles the life**. Airtight jar + desiccant + cellar
is the best granary there is. And **grow everything out at least every 3 years**: seed never sown is
not seed, it is a souvenir.

**Open-pollinated varieties only**: F1 hybrids do not come true from saved seed.

## 8 · Time and hands — the number nobody wants to see

| Task by hand | Rate |
|---|---|
| reaping with a sickle | ~500 m²/day/person |
| threshing with a flail | 50–100 kg grain/day |
| **grinding on a hand mill** | **~1 kg of flour per hour** |
| splitting and stacking firewood | 1–1.5 stère/day for two people |
| carrying water | 20 L per trip (200 L/day = **10 round trips**) |
| milking a cow | 2 × 20 min, **365 days a year, no exceptions** |

A family of four eats ~2 kg of flour a day: **two hours of grinding, every day**. A powered mill, a
millstone, a donkey or a waterwheel are not comforts — they are **working days handed back** to you.
One good hand mill or bicycle generator beats ten gadgets.

**Overall order of magnitude**: feeding a family in full self-reliance is **about one and a half
full-time jobs**, year round. It is not a weekend project.

## 9 · Group and distance

| Group size | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 household (2–5 people) | **one broken leg or one flu and everything stops.** Not viable beyond a few months |
| 3–5 households (15–25 people) | illness, night watch, milking and heavy work are all covered |
| 30–60 people | you can support a specialist: blacksmith, midwife, miller |
| 150 | the ceiling of direct trust (Dunbar) — beyond it you need written rules |

**Moving without fuel**: on foot 25–30 km/day with 15 kg · bicycle and trailer 40–80 kg over
30–60 km/day · draught horse ~30 km/day with 100 kg, and it eats 2 ha.
**Direct consequence**: your food, water and firewood must fit inside a **5 km radius**. Beyond that,
it is not your resource.

## 10 · One year of stores — the short list

Per **person** per **year**, for the case where nothing grows. All of it keeps 5 years or more, cool and dry.

| Item | Quantity |
|---|---|
| grain (wheat, rice, oats) | **180 kg** |
| dry pulses | 30 kg |
| **fats** (oil, lard, ghee) | **25 kg** — *the hardest to store long: rotate every 2 years* |
| sugar / honey | 20 kg |
| **salt** | **5 kg** (store 10) |
| milk powder | 15 kg |
| yeast, baking soda, vinegar, spices | 5 kg |

≈ **280 kg per person**, i.e. **~1.1 t for a household of four**: about **2 m³**, one small cool dry
room. Add a hand mill, 10 kg of soap or the fat to make it, cooking fuel, and a way to filter water.
**Rotate the stock** — a store never eaten is a store never checked.

## Redo these calculations at home

```
Rainwater (L/yr)    = roof footprint (m²) × rainfall (mm/yr) × 0.8
Cistern (L)         = people × L/day × days in the longest dry spell
Calories needed     = people × 3 500 kcal × 365
Garden area         = kcal needed ÷ kcal per m² of the crop (potato ≈ 1 500 kcal/m²)
Firewood (stères)   = heat needed (kWh) ÷ 1 400
Woodland (ha)       = stères/year ÷ 5
Solar (kWp)         = winter kWh/day ÷ 0.7   (December yield)
Battery (kWh)       = kWh/day × 3 ÷ depth of discharge (0.8 LiFePO4 / 0.5 lead)
Grain for hens      = hens × 45 kg/year
```

**Write your real numbers in the margin during year one.** Yours are worth more than mine.
